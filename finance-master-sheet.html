My approach

How I invest

The template is built around how I manage my own money. Here's the thinking behind it.

Consistency beats the perfect strategy

Investing the same amount every month, whatever the price, matters more than finding the perfect portfolio. This is called dollar-cost averaging, and the Brokerage tab is built around it. A simple plan you stick to for years will do more for you than a clever one you abandon.

Never sell

I buy and hold for the long term. Selling in a panic locks in losses, and trying to time the market usually means missing its best days.

A falling market is an opportunity

When the market or the S&P 500 goes down, I see it as a chance to buy more at lower prices. I believe the market goes up in the long run, and the S&P 500 is designed that way: weaker companies drop out of the index and stronger ones replace them.

Historically, the S&P 500 has recovered from every crash and gone on to new highs, although some recoveries have taken years.

I recommend holding gold

Gold tends to hold its value when stocks are struggling, so it balances out a stock-heavy portfolio.

US and Ireland-domiciled funds

I hold both US-domiciled funds (like VOO) and Ireland-domiciled UCITS funds (like SPYL) for tax reasons. For many investors outside the US, Ireland-domiciled funds mean less tax taken from dividends and no exposure to US estate tax. Check what applies in your country.

You don't have to copy what I do

This is what works for me, at my age and in my situation. Use the template however suits you: change the buckets, the targets and the categories. The best plan is the one you'll actually stick with.
